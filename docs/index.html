10 Conclusión del Día — Para Principiantes click to expand ›
📋 Resumen del día

Monday, August 3, 2026 delivered a confusing but important day for markets — stocks climbed, gold hit new highs, oil crashed, and bond yields spiked all at the same time. The biggest story under the surface is that bonds and stocks are disagreeing: the stock market is acting like everything is fine, but the bond market is quietly raising a red flag by pushing long-term interest rates higher. When these two markets disagree, it usually means a resolution is coming — and it's not always the stock market that wins.

⭐ Lo más importante
  • Oil crashed 6%+ on Middle East diplomacy — energy stocks face earnings cuts and the inflation picture just got less scary, giving the Fed more flexibility
  • 10-Year Treasury yield jumped to 4.745% — this is the "price of money" for the whole economy, and when it rises this fast, it makes stocks relatively less attractive and loans more expensive for everyone
  • USD/JPY fell 2.11% in one day — this yen move is unusual and could signal that Japan's central bank intervened, which would unwind a massive global trade called the "carry trade" and create ripple effects across markets
💡 Concepto del día

Today's concept is the **"Geopolitical Risk Premium"** — this is extra money that traders add to the price of oil, gold, or other assets when they're worried about wars or political crises disrupting supply. Think of it like paying extra for travel insurance before a trip to a risky destination. When Trump's diplomatic news reduced the perceived danger in the Middle East, traders immediately removed that "insurance premium" from oil prices — which is why crude fell so sharply without any change in actual supply or demand.

👀 Para mañana

Watch for any Fed official speeches on Tuesday that respond to the 10-year yield spike — if policymakers sound alarmed, markets could reprice quickly. Also monitor whether the USD/JPY stabilizes above 155 or continues falling, as a sustained yen rally would signal the unwinding of global carry trades, which is the hidden financial plumbing that keeps many risk assets inflated.
