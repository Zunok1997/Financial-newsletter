📋 Resumen del día

Today's market sent a mixed but important message — stocks mostly rose thanks to strong earnings from big tech companies, but underneath the surface, a serious warning signal is flashing: oil is above $110 a barrel because Iran has not reopened the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping lane. Gold is at record highs near $4,627 and the U.S. dollar is weakening, which together suggest that sophisticated investors are quietly preparing for a world where inflation stays high and economic growth slows — a dangerous combination called stagflation.

⭐ Lo más importante
  • Iran Hormuz blockade — it is keeping oil prices dangerously high, which raises costs for everything from gasoline to groceries, threatening both consumers and the Federal Reserve's ability to cut interest rates
  • Magnificent 7 earnings beat — the biggest tech companies proved their AI investments are paying off, which is the main reason stocks didn't fall today despite the oil shock
  • Gold at $4,627 and Dollar weakening — when gold surges and the dollar falls together, it means big investors are losing confidence in the financial system's stability, a rare and serious warning sign
💡 Concepto del día

Today's key concept is **stagflation** — a painful economic condition where inflation stays high (prices keep rising) while economic growth slows down at the same time. Think of it like driving a car with the engine overheating while you're also running out of gas — the normal fix (raise rates to cool inflation OR cut rates to boost growth) doesn't work cleanly because both problems fight against each other. The oil shock from the Hormuz blockade is a classic stagflation trigger, exactly like the 1970s oil crisis.

👀 Para mañana

The single most important event in the next 24 hours is Friday's PCE inflation report — this is the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge, and if it comes in hotter than expected (which rising oil prices make likely), it could push back any hope of interest rate cuts and send bond yields higher and stocks lower. Also watch for any diplomatic news on the Iran-U.S. standoff over the weekend, as any hint of Hormuz reopening would be an immediate relief catalyst for markets.
