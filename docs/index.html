📋 Resumen del día

Today was a broad "risk-off" day where stocks fell across the board as bond yields climbed toward a key psychological level, the U.S. dollar strengthened, and commodities sold off together — a combination that signals investors are worried about inflation staying stubbornly high while economic growth slows. The one shining exception was Intel, which surged 9% on news of an Apple partnership, reminding us that even in tough market days, individual catalysts can create powerful opportunities.

⭐ Lo más importante
  • 10Y Treasury yield rising to 4.487% — when borrowing costs rise, stocks become relatively less attractive because investors can earn more from "safe" bonds instead
  • Intel-Apple domestic chip partnership — this is a rare case of government policy and corporate strategy aligning to create a genuine business catalyst, not just headlines
  • USD/JPY at 160.91 — the Japanese yen is at its weakest level in years, and Japan's central bank has intervened in currency markets at these levels before, which could create sudden volatility overnight
💡 Concepto del día

**Yield Curve Steepening**: Today the gap between short-term (3M at 3.62%) and long-term (10Y at 4.49%) U.S. interest rates widened — this is called a "steepening yield curve." Think of it like this: if a bank charges you more interest for a 30-year mortgage than a 1-year loan, that's normal — but when that gap grows suddenly, it often means bond investors are demanding extra compensation for holding long-term debt, usually because they're worried about government deficits or sticky inflation. When this happens, it typically puts downward pressure on growth stocks because their future profits get discounted more heavily.
