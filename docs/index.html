📋 Resumen del día

Today's market was dominated by one giant headline — reports that the U.S. and Iran are close to a nuclear deal — which sent oil prices crashing more than 10% while stocks, gold, and bonds all rallied together. It's a rare "everything is fine" day where falling energy prices act like a tax cut for consumers and businesses, easing inflation fears and giving investors more reasons to be optimistic about the economy.

⭐ Lo más importante
  • Oil crashed -11% on Iran deal reports — cheaper energy is like a pay raise for every business and household, and it reduces inflation pressure that was keeping the Fed cautious about cutting interest rates.
  • AMD's earnings beat drove the Nasdaq higher — it confirmed that the AI investment boom is still very real, and technology companies building the infrastructure for artificial intelligence are delivering on their promises.
  • Gold hit $4,726 and silver surged nearly 7% — precious metals rising sharply while stocks also rise is unusual and tells us that smart institutional investors are hedging against uncertainty even while participating in the rally.
💡 Concepto del día

**Reflexivity** (George Soros's concept): Sometimes markets move based on what people *expect* to happen, not what has actually happened yet — and those expectations change reality. Today, the mere *report* of an Iran deal (not a confirmed deal) caused oil to crash 11%, which itself will reduce inflation, which may cause the Fed to cut rates sooner, which makes stocks more attractive — a chain reaction started by a single news article. Think of it like a crowd starting to run toward the exit: the movement itself *becomes* the event, regardless of whether there was actually a fire.

Watch for formal U.S. government confirmation or denial of the Iran nuclear deal within the next 24-48 hours — a confirmation would extend today's rally in bonds and stocks while crushing energy further, but a denial could trigger a sharp oil rebound and risk-off reversal. Also monitor weekly jobless claims data and any Fed speaker commentary on whether the deflationary impact of lower oil changes their rate cut calculus.
