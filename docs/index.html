📋 Resumen del día

Today's market is being driven by one big story: Trump and Xi meeting face-to-face in Beijing, which gave investors enough confidence to push Nasdaq up over 1% and European markets higher. At the same time, the bond market is quietly flashing a warning sign — long-term interest rates keep rising, with the 30-year Treasury now at 5%, telling us that inflation is not going away as fast as everyone hoped.

⭐ Lo más importante
  • Trump-Xi Beijing Summit — this is the most important geopolitical event in months because even a small step toward less tension between the world's two largest economies reduces the risk of shocks to global trade, tech, and energy markets.
  • 30-Year Treasury yield at 5.047% — when long-term borrowing costs rise this high, it becomes more expensive for companies and governments to borrow money, which eventually slows growth and puts pressure on stock valuations, especially high-growth tech stocks.
  • Physical crude oil premiums collapsing despite headline price above $100 — this hidden divergence means the real oil market is not as tight as the scary Hormuz headlines suggest, and a sudden drop in crude prices could hurt energy stocks while benefiting consumers and airlines.
💡 Concepto del día

Today's concept is the **"Thucydides Trap"** — an idea from history that says when a rising power (China) threatens to overtake a dominant power (USA), the result is often war, not because either side wants it, but because of fear and miscalculation. Think of it like two big kids on a playground, both afraid the other will throw the first punch, so tensions keep escalating. The fact that Xi brought this concept up in the meeting is actually a hopeful sign — he's saying "let's be smart and not fall into this trap," which markets interpreted as a desire for stability rather than confrontation.

👀 Para mañana

The most important thing to watch in the next 24-48 hours is any official statement or joint communiqué coming out of the Trump-Xi summit — specific language on tariffs, Taiwan, or semiconductors could move markets sharply in either direction. Also watch for any Fed speakers reacting to today's rising yields, as their tone on inflation will set the stage for next week's risk appetite.
