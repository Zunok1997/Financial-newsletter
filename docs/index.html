The biggest macro wildcard is President Trump's claim that an Iran nuclear deal is days away, which sent oil prices sharply lower and could be the most important economic event of the week if confirmed.

⭐ Lo más importante
  • AI chip rally — Nasdaq's outperformance signals that institutional money is betting heavily on the AI infrastructure supercycle continuing, making semiconductor stocks the single most important sector to watch
  • Iran deal potential — A Hormuz reopening would lower gasoline and energy costs globally, acting like a surprise tax cut for consumers and businesses at exactly the moment they need it most
  • 30-Year Treasury at 5% — When long-term government borrowing costs hit this level, it puts pressure on home loans, corporate debt, and stock valuations simultaneously, making it a quiet but powerful risk to monitor
💡 Concepto del día

**The Risk Premium** — Think of every investment as having a "worry surcharge" built into its price; when Iran threatens oil routes, that surcharge on oil prices goes up, and when a deal is reached, the surcharge disappears and prices fall. Today's oil drop of over $2 is almost entirely the Iran risk premium evaporating — not because oil became less useful, but because traders stopped charging you extra for the possibility of a shipping crisis. Understanding that prices contain both "what something is worth" and "how worried people are" is one of the most powerful lenses in investing.
