📋 Resumen del día

Today's session was dominated by one word: geopolitics. President Trump declared the U.S.-Iran ceasefire "over," sending oil prices surging over 5% while global equity markets sold off broadly — think of it as the market repricing risk across the board when a major uncertainty suddenly gets much bigger. The Nasdaq bore the brunt of the selling (-1.16%) while the Hang Seng was the only major index to rise (+2.99%), signaling that China is beginning to trade on its own story rather than following the global mood.

⭐ Lo más importante
  • Iran ceasefire collapse — oil spiking 5% means higher energy costs for everything, from airline tickets to groceries, reigniting inflation fears just when the Fed wanted to cut rates.
  • Apple's $30B Broadcom chip deal — this is the largest American manufacturing commitment Apple has ever made, and it tells you that the "make chips in America" trend has real money behind it, not just political speeches.
  • Fed minutes release today — if new Fed Chair Warsh signals he wants to communicate less with markets, investors lose the "map" they've used for years to navigate interest rate decisions, and that uncertainty alone can push stocks lower.
💡 Concepto del día

Today's concept is the **Geopolitical Risk Premium** — this is the extra return investors demand for holding assets when war, political instability, or sanctions create unpredictable outcomes. Think of it like buying a house on a street where a neighbor might build a factory: you'd want to pay less for that house because of the uncertainty, and you'd demand a higher return to compensate for the risk. Today, that premium spiked in oil and bond markets the moment Trump's ceasefire announcement hit the wires.

👀 Para mañana

Watch two things closely in the next 24-48 hours: first, how oil prices behave at the $78-80 Brent level — if they push through $80, expect a second wave of inflation repricing that hits bonds, growth stocks, and consumer spending expectations simultaneously. Second, read the Fed minutes carefully for any sign that the central bank is becoming less communicative, because a quieter Fed combined with a louder Middle East is exactly the combination that historically produces the most volatile market weeks.
